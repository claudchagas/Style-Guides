## 1. Overview & Purpose

This guide defines the linguistic and formatting standards for translating Eurail and Interrail content from English into Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR). It applies to all content published on eurail.com and associated whitelabel partner sites.

**Scope:** Website UI, product pages, inspirational content, legal pages, emails, social media, maps, and any other customer-facing material.

**Linguistic authority:** Apply rules in this guide first. For any case not covered here, consult the following reference works in order:

1. Novo Aurélio: Dicionário da Língua Portuguesa — Século XXI
2. Michaelis: Moderno Dicionário da Língua Portuguesa
3. Dicionário Houaiss da Língua Portuguesa
4. Dicionário UNESP da Língua Portuguesa
5. Post-1990 Orthographic Agreement (Acordo Ortográfico)

## 2. Target Audience & Use Cases

**Audience profile:** Non-European travelers who are largely unfamiliar with both train travel and European geography. The audience spans a wide age range — from college students to retirees to business travelers. Do not assume any prior knowledge of railway systems or European countries.

**Reading level:** Grade 8 (standard/industry-specific). Accessible to a broad general audience.

**Content types and approach:**

| Content type | Approach |
|---|---|
| Informative content (train info, product info) | Stay close to the source text. Prioritize comprehension for an audience unfamiliar with train travel. |
| Inspirational content (itineraries, top-10 pages) | Allow more creative latitude. Convey enthusiasm and a sense of discovery. |
| Legal and official pages (see list below) | Use formal register; no contractions. |

**Legal pages requiring formal register:**

- Conditions of Use
- Booking Terms
- Privacy Policy
- Disclaimer
- Return Policy
- Service Policy

> This list may expand. Any page designated as "official" by the project team requires formal register.

## 3. Tone & Register

### 3.1 General register

Use an **informal, friendly, and light** tone that conveys a "happy holiday" atmosphere. Address readers using **"você" / "vocês"** throughout — never "tu".

**Do:** Warm, welcoming, accessible.
**Don't:** Slang, regional colloquialisms, or vocabulary that would alienate older readers or first-time travelers.

Colloquialisms are acceptable only when all three conditions are met:
- Established by widespread use in Brazil
- Listed in a standard Portuguese dictionary (e.g., "dica" for "tip")
- Broadly understood across age groups

### 3.2 Register by content type

**Standard content:** Informal. Contractions permitted.

**Legal pages:** Formal. No contractions. Follow the same capitalization conventions as the English source.

### 3.3 Active vs. passive voice

Prefer the active voice to achieve a direct, engaging style. When the source text uses passive voice, choose whichever form — active or passive — produces the most natural and direct result in pt-BR.

## 4. Terminology & Preferred Translations

### 4.1 Product name rules

Product names beginning with "Eurail" or "Interrail" are proper names subject to strict rules. **Never translate product names without following the decision table below.**

#### Decision table — "Pass" and product name handling

| Source text pattern | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| "Eurail Pass" / "Interrail Pass" (both words capitalized) | Keep entirely in English, capitalized | Eurail Pass |
| "Eurail pass" (only "pass" lowercase) | Keep "Eurail"; translate "pass" → "passe" | passe Eurail |
| Full product name (e.g., "Eurail Italy Pass Adult") | Keep brand + country; translate and capitalize audience modifier | Eurail Itália Pass Adulto |
| "Global Pass" / "One Country Pass" (pass-type names) | Keep in English, all words capitalized | Global Pass / One Country Pass |
| "Pass" / "a Pass" / "your Pass" (generic reference to a Eurail/Interrail pass) | Translate; capitalize | Passe / um Passe / seu Passe |
| "pass" (generic, lowercase, not referring to a specific product) | Translate lowercase; reverse word order | passe Itália / passes Itália |

> **Critical distinction:** "pass" ≠ "ticket". A pass covers multiple journeys; a ticket covers a single journey between two points. Translate accordingly: **pass = passe / ticket = passagem**.

#### Stripping "train/rail" from certain product names

The following product names drop the word "train" or "rail" in the translation:

| Source | Translation |
|---|---|
| Eurail One Country Rail Pass | Eurail One Country Pass |
| Eurail Global Train Pass | Eurail Global Pass |

#### Pass types

Pass types may be translated into pt-BR. On the **first mention**, place the English name in brackets after the translation. Subsequent mentions may use the pt-BR form alone.

| English | pt-BR |
|---|---|
| Child Pass | Passe Infantil |
| Youth Pass | Passe Jovem |
| Adult Pass | Passe Adulto |
| Continuous Pass | Passe Contínuo |
| Promotional Pass | Passes promocionais |
| Saver Pass | *(no official translation — see glossary)* |
| Flexi Pass | *(no official translation — see glossary)* |

#### Other important terms

These terms may be translated without special conditions. Always capitalize them.

| English | pt-BR |
|---|---|
| Pass Cover | Capa do Passe |
| Pass holder | Portador do Passe |
| Pass benefits | Vantagens do Passe |
| Travel Diary | Diário de Viagem |
| Travel Calendar | Calendário de Viagem |
| Eurail Aid Office | Serviço de Assistência ao Cliente Eurail |
| Rail Planner App | O aplicativo Rail Planner |

> **Rail Planner:** Always capitalize. Do **not** translate "Rail Planner". "App" may be translated.

> **"Create your own story":** When used as a title — keep in English in blackflower font with the pt-BR translation beneath. When embedded in body text — on first mention, place the English in brackets after the pt-BR; subsequent mentions may use either form.

### 4.2 Fees — reservation vs. booking

Maintain a clear and consistent distinction between these two terms in every job:

| Term | Definition | Who charges it |
|---|---|---|
| Reservation fee | Fee for reserving a seat on the train | Railway carrier |
| Booking fee | Fee for booking through the Eurail/Interrail website | Eurail / Interrail |

These must be translated in a way that keeps them **visibly distinct** in the target text. Confirm the approved pt-BR renderings in the project glossary.

### 4.3 Trademarks and slogans

Keep trademarked names and slogans in English unless the client has provided an official pt-BR translation or explicit written instructions to translate.

### 4.4 Forbidden terms

The following must never appear in any translation, regardless of source text:

| Forbidden | Reason |
|---|---|
| "seguro" as a translation of "coverage" in "Pass Loss & Theft Cover/Coverage" | Legal restriction |
| The acronym "LTC" | Legal restriction |
| Referring to InterRailnet.com as the "official website" for European rail passes | Contractual restriction |

A full list of forbidden words and phrases is maintained in the project glossary.

### 4.5 Proper names, titles, and institutions

| Category | Rule |
|---|---|
| Company names (e.g., Eurail Group G.I.E.) | Do not translate |
| Personal titles (Mr., Dr.) | Translate: Sr. / Sra. / Dr. / Dra. |
| Job titles (manager, director) | Translate: gerente, presidente, diretor |
| Departments and laws | Translate |
| Agencies and institutions | Translate if a widely accepted pt-BR equivalent exists |
| US state names | Do not translate (California = California) |

## 5. Grammar & Syntax Conventions

### 5.1 Voice

Prefer active voice. When the source uses passive voice, select whichever construction produces the most direct and natural result in pt-BR.

### 5.2 Abbreviations

Avoid abbreviations whenever possible. Content must be readable for all audience types, including readers with no prior knowledge of the subject. Use abbreviations only when:

- Space constraints make the full form unworkable, and
- All other options have been exhausted.

When abbreviations are used, follow standard Portuguese abbreviation conventions. Example: "info" → "informação/informações" (if space allows); otherwise → "inf."

### 5.3 Acronyms

If an acronym appears in the source text, the translation must also include an acronym. Apply the following pt-BR rules:

- **First mention per page:** Write the full term followed by the acronym in parentheses.
  - Example: Banco Nacional de Desenvolvimento Econômico (BNDE)
- **Subsequent mentions:** Use the acronym alone.
- **4+ letters, each pronounced separately:** All capitals — e.g., BNDE.
- **Pronounceable as a word:** First letter only capitalized — e.g., Fepasa.

### 5.4 Foreign words

Use judgment. A foreign word in the source text may be rendered in the target as:

- Italics only: *joie-de-vivre*
- Italics + translation in brackets: *pédalo* (pedalinho)
- Translation only, no italics

Do **not** use italics for foreign words that have been absorbed into the pt-BR lexicon and appear in standard dictionaries.

### 5.5 Buttons and calls to action

- **Default form:** Imperative verb. No punctuation marks except question marks where present in the source.
- **Fallback when space is limited:** Infinitive.

| Source | Preferred | Space-limited fallback |
|---|---|---|
| View prices | Verifique preços / Veja preços | Ver preços |

### 5.6 Hyperlinks

When the hyperlink text and the title of the destination page match in the source, the translation of both must also match.

## 6. Punctuation, Capitalization & Formatting

### 6.1 Capitalization

**Standard rules:**

- Sentence case throughout: capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.
- Headings: first word capitalized only. Headings must be consistent across the site.
- No uppercase after a colon unless followed by a proper name.
- Days and months: lowercase.
- Do not capitalize job titles: presidente, diretor, diretor executivo.
- Capitalize department names: Departamento de Marketing.

**Legal pages:** Follow the English source capitalization.

**URLs:** No accents, special characters, or uppercase letters.

> ✅ `eurail.com/visita-espana-manana`
> ❌ `eurail.com/Visita-España-Mañana`

**Reference:** [Estadão — Maiúsculas e Minúsculas](http://www.estadao-escola.com.br/manualredacao/esclareca/maiusculaseminusculas.shtm)

### 6.2 Punctuation

**Exclamation marks:** Use sparingly. English uses exclamation marks far more frequently than Portuguese. In pt-BR, reserve them for genuine excitement, surprise, or interjections: Ah!, Oh!, Avante!, Viva!

**Spacing:** One space after: period, comma, semicolon, colon, question mark, exclamation mark.

**Colon:** No capital letter after a colon unless followed by a proper name.

**Dashes:**
- En dash (–): Use in place of a hyphen where the source uses a hyphen as a dash. Encode as `&ndash;` in HTML. In most cases a comma may also be used.
- Do not introduce em dashes (—) unless present in the source.

**Quotation marks:**
- Primary: double quotation marks `" "`.
- Nested citation (a quote within a quote): single quotation marks `' '`.

**Ordinal numbers:** Always use superscript.

> ✅ 1ª classe / 2ª classe / 1º de janeiro
> ❌ 1a classe / 1o de janeiro

### 6.3 Lists (bulleted and numbered)

- Introduce every list with an explanatory sentence ending in a colon `:`.
- List items that are complete sentences: capitalize first word, end with a full stop.
- List items that are fragments: capitalize first word, no closing punctuation — except the **last item**, which ends with a full stop.

## 7. Numbers, Dates, Times, Currencies & Units

### 7.1 Numbers

| Element | Convention | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Decimal separator | Comma, no space | 1.000,00 |
| Thousands separator | Period | 1.000 |
| Ordinals | Superscript ª / º | 1ª classe / 1º de janeiro |

### 7.2 Dates

**Standard format:** Day + month (lowercase) + year.

> ✅ 20 de junho de 2012
> ✅ 20/06/2012 or 20-06-2012

**Calendar abbreviations:**

| Weekdays (standard) | seg. / ter. / qua. / qui. / sex. / sáb. / dom. |
|---|---|
| Weekdays (space-limited) | seg / ter / qua / qui / sex / sáb / dom |
| Weekdays (extreme space) | S / T / Q / Q / S / S / D |
| Months (standard) | jan. / fev. / mar. / abr. / maio / jun. / jul. / ago. / set. / out. / nov. / dez. |
| Months (space-limited) | jan / fev / mar / abr / mai / jun / jul / ago / set / out / nov / dez |

### 7.3 Times

Mirror the source format (12-hour or 24-hour) but always use a colon `:` as the separator — never a period.

> ✅ 3:30 / 19:15
> ❌ 3.30 / 19.15

### 7.4 Currencies

- Place the currency symbol **before** the number, separated by a **non-breaking space**.
- Use a period for thousands and a comma for decimals.
- Keep the `$` symbol when it appears in the source; add the currency code before it if required.

### 7.5 Units of measurement

- The metric value is always listed first.
- If the source includes both metric and imperial, place the imperial value in parentheses after the metric value.
- Numerical values are handled by the CAT tool (Webcatt); adapt only where the tool does not apply.

## 8. Geographic & Place Name Conventions

### 8.1 Countries and cities

- Translate all country names using the official pt-BR form. Maintain consistency (see glossary for variant spellings).
  - Example: Bósnia-Herzegóvina / EUA / Grã-Bretanha / Macedônia
- Do **not** translate US state names.
  - Example: California = California

### 8.2 Places and attractions

On **first mention**, write the pt-BR name followed by the local-language name in parentheses.

> ✅ Basílica de São Pedro (Basilica Papale di San Pietro)
> ✅ Viena (Wien)

If an attraction has no pt-BR name, use the local name with a Portuguese description:

> ✅ Keukenhof – o maior jardim do mundo

On longer pages, repeat this pattern on the first instance in each new text block if that block is linked to from elsewhere on the page.

### 8.3 Maps and directions

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Country names on maps | Always translate |
| City name on map in local script with English name underneath (e.g., Praha / PRAGUE) | Keep the **local** name only — do not translate |
| City name appears once (same in local + English, e.g., Paris) | Translate if pt-BR has a different form (Madri); otherwise keep source |

If space is insufficient, contact the Eurail project team.

## 9. URL Conventions

- Translate URL slugs into pt-BR.
- Use **all lowercase** — no uppercase letters.
- Use **hyphens** as separators.
- **No** accent marks or special characters in URLs.
- Remove stop words (common function words filtered by search engines).

> ✅ `eurail.com/viagem-de-trem-na-europa`
> ❌ `eurail.com/Viagem-de-Trem-na-Europa`
> ❌ `eurail.com/viagem-de-trem-na-európa`

## 10. Space & Length Constraints

**Headings:** Be as concise as possible. Aim to keep headings on a single line.

**Abbreviations:** Only as a last resort when space constraints cannot be solved any other way.

**Buttons/CTAs:** If the imperative form exceeds the available space, use the infinitive.

If a space issue cannot be resolved within these guidelines, contact the Eurail project team before delivering.

## 11. Do's and Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Use "você / vocês" to address the reader | Use "tu" |
| Keep product names in English and capitalized: Eurail Italy Pass | Translate product names: *Passe Eurail Itália* |
| Translate "pass" when lowercase: passe Eurail | Translate "pass" as "ticket" |
| Use imperative for buttons: "Verifique preços" | Add punctuation to button labels: "Verifique preços." |
| Keep country names consistent with the glossary: Bélgica, Países Baixos | Use inconsistent or informal country names |
| Write ordinals in superscript: 1ª, 2º | Write ordinals as plain text: 1a, 2o |
| Use a colon as the time separator: 19:15 | Use a period as the time separator: 19.15 |
| Comma for decimals, period for thousands: 1.000,50 | Period for decimals, comma for thousands: 1,000.50 |
| Keep URLs lowercase, hyphenated, accent-free | Use accented characters or uppercase in URLs |
| Maintain the reservation fee / booking fee distinction | Use either term interchangeably |
| Keep "Pass Loss & Theft Coverage" out of "seguro" | Translate "coverage" as "seguro" in this context |
| Use formal register on all legal pages | Apply informal register to Conditions of Use, Privacy Policy, etc. |
| Italicize or bracket foreign words absorbed into PT-BR | Automatically mirror source italics for all foreign words |
