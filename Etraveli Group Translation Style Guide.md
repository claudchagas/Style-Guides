## 1. Overview & purpose

This guide defines the linguistic and formatting standards for translating Etraveli Group / GoToGate content from U.S. English into Brazilian Portuguese (pt-BR). It applies to all customer-facing copy — UI strings, microcopy, email templates, newsletters, landing pages, product descriptions, and marketing materials — published under the GoToGate brand.

The goal is consistency across all touchpoints: a translator or reviewer picking up any GoToGate string should make the same choices independently.

## 2. Brand overview

### 2.1 Etraveli Group

Etraveli Group is a world-leading tech platform for selling flights. It holds a dominant position in the UK and European markets — over 30% OTA market share in the Nordics — and processes €3.0bn annually across 70+ countries. Its consumer brands are **Mytrip**, **Gotogate**, and **Flight Network**.

### 2.2 GoToGate

GoToGate is Etraveli Group's flagship B2C online travel agency. It operates in 77 countries across 6 continents, with close to 14 million customers. Brand promise: **Fly smart, enjoy more**.

GoToGate's audience skews slightly older and more sophisticated than Mytrip — families, couples, ages 35–50. They are educated, price-sensitive but not exclusively budget-driven: smart buyers, explorers, and interest/event-based travelers.

### 2.3 Brand naming rules

- Write the brand as **GoToGate** — one word, capital initials. Never "Go To Gate" or "gotogate".
- **Mytrip** and **Gotogate** are always written as one word.
- **Flight Network** is always two words.
- The shorthand **GTG** is acceptable in casual communications and social media only.
- Do not translate brand names under any circumstances.

## 4. Tone & register

### 4.1 GoToGate tone of voice

The GoToGate tone applies to all Etraveli Group brands. It has seven qualities:

**Descriptive** — Describe feelings and experiences vividly, but not poetically or lyrically. Metaphors are welcome when used simply and directly.

**Personal** — Write in the first and second person. Avoid the third person ("GoToGate wishes you a happy flight"). Do not be intimate or invasive.

**Relaxed and informal** — Contractions are encouraged (we're, you'll, it's, don't). The tone is casual and friendly, not overly formal. Avoid self-inflation or intimacy.

**Uncomplicated** — Short sentences, common vocabulary, clear meaning. One idea per sentence.

**Factual** — Use facts and figures to make the point. Ground claims in concrete data.

**Reassuring** — Use more formal language when asking customers to trust you — for security notices, formal qualifications, or legal content.

**Youthful** — Fresh, energized vocabulary within the limits of a professional tone. Do not be provocative.

**Unembellished** — Few adjectives. Avoid unusual or over-elaborate words.

### 4.2 Tone by content type

| Content type | Register | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| UI copy / microcopy / transactional | Formal | Stay close to source. Active/imperative voice. No contractions in error messages or legal strings. |
| Customer emails (informational) | Semi-formal | Clear, direct, reassuring. Use first name when available. |
| Newsletters / promotional copy | Informal | Contractions encouraged. Emojis acceptable when appropriate. Light exclamation marks permitted. |
| Landing pages / marketing copy | Informal | Transcreation permitted for creative/promotional text. |
| Legal / security notices | Formal | Stay close to source. No contractions. |
| B2B / corporate content | Formal | Industry terms acceptable. Formal register. |

### 4.3 Translation vs. transcreation

**Informational, instructional, or transactional copy** — Stay close to the source. Prioritize accuracy, clarity, and consistency with established UI terminology.

**Promotional or creative copy** — Transcreation is permitted. Prioritize tone, cultural fit, and persuasive impact over literal fidelity. When departing significantly from the source, note the approach in the Smartling comment field.

> Avoid culturally specific expressions, idioms, or wordplay that will not work in Brazilian Portuguese. GoToGate copy is translated into many languages; humor and wordplay that works in English may not survive translation.

## 6. Grammar & syntax

### 6.1 Voice

Use the active voice throughout. It produces shorter, clearer, more engaging sentences.

For instructional copy, use the imperative:

> ✅ "Verifique a válvula e instale-a novamente."
> ❌ "A válvula deve ser verificada antes de ser instalada novamente."

Use passive voice only where it is genuinely more natural in pt-BR or where the active form would be awkward.

### 6.2 Abbreviations

Avoid abbreviations. When an abbreviation is unavoidable (space constraints, established industry acronym), introduce the full term first:

> Write "Agência de Viagens Online" before using "OTA". Write "Termos e Condições" before using "T&C".

### 6.3 Contractions

Contractions are encouraged in informal and semi-formal contexts. Default to using them unless the content is formal (legal notices, analyses, reports).

### 6.4 Gender-neutral language

Use inclusive, gender-neutral language throughout. Avoid "ele/ela" or "seu/sua" when a gender-neutral alternative is available.

Use plural forms of subject nouns with plural pronouns where possible:

> ✅ "O CEO convidou os candidatos a trazerem seus portfólios para análise."
> ❌ "O CEO o convidou a trazer seu portfólio para que ela pudesse analisá-lo."

## 7. Punctuation, capitalization & formatting

### 7.1 Punctuation rules

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Em dashes (—) | Do not use in translations |
| En dashes (–) | Use for ranges: numbers, dates, times ("Capítulos 5–8"; "2015–2019") |
| Semicolons | Avoid |
| Hashtags | Avoid |
| Asterisks | Avoid |
| Exclamation marks | Use sparingly — newsletters and promotional copy only |
| Emojis | Social media and newsletters only, when appropriate |
| Oxford comma | Do not use — no comma before the final "and" or "or" in a list, unless required for clarity |
| Single quotation marks | Only to enclose a quote within a quote |
| Double quotation marks | All other uses: direct quotes, special terms, named sections |

### 7.2 Capitalization

Apply standard pt-BR rules:

- Sentence case for all headings, UI labels, and body copy — capitalize only the first word and proper nouns.
- Title case for newsletter headings and some social media copy, where specified.
- Do not capitalize job titles mid-sentence.
- Days of the week, months, languages, and nationalities: lowercase.

### 7.3 Hyphens

Use hyphens in:

- Compound adjectives: "viagem de ida e volta", "voo direto", "tarifa econômica"
- Compound adjectives with numbers: "itinerário de 10 dias", "voo de 3 horas"
- Date and time ranges: "segunda-feira–sexta-feira", "junho–julho"

Do not hyphenate after common prefixes (pre-, post-, anti-, co-) unless clarity requires it.

## 8. Numbers, dates, times, currencies & units

### 8.1 Numbers

All numbers as digits — do not spell them out:

> ✅ "Minhas férias começam em 14 dias."
> ❌ "Minhas férias começam em quatorze dias."

### 8.2 Percentages

Use the % symbol — do not write "por cento".

> ✅ "A satisfação dos clientes aumentou 20%."

No space between the number and % symbol:

> ✅ "20%" ❌ "20 %"

### 8.3 Units of measurement

Include a single space between the number and the abbreviated unit:

> ✅ "2 mm" ❌ "2mm"

### 8.4 Dates

Use the standard pt-BR format: day + month (written out, lowercase) + year.

> ✅ 15 de maio de 2025 / 15/05/2025

### 8.5 Times

24-hour clock with colon as separator:

> ✅ "Chegamos na terça-feira às 14:00."
> ❌ "Chegamos na terça-feira às 2:00 pm."

### 8.6 Currencies

- Currency code before the amount: R$ 50 / USD 50 / EUR 40 — not "50 USD".
- Use consistent currency codes across the same text.
- Decimal: period — R$ 1.50.
- Thousands: comma — R$ 1,000.
- When multiple currencies appear, always specify each: "USD 50 / EUR 40".

The following must remain in English exactly as they appear in the source:

| Category | Notes |
|---|---|
| Website names | All domain names and URLs |
| Brand names | GoToGate, Mytrip, Flight Network, Etraveli Group, Precision |
| Product names | All Etraveli Group product and feature names |
| Usernames | Any user handle or identifier |
| Email addresses | user@domain.com |
| URLs | Do not localize URLs into Portuguese |

## 10. UI/UX & product-specific conventions

### 10.1 Software strings

Translate UI elements (buttons, menu items, labels) as you would any other text — unless the Smartling job specifies otherwise. Stay as close to the source as possible in both meaning and length. Use the imperative form for buttons and CTAs.

### 10.2 Accessibility

Apply the following WCAG principles in all translations:

**Plain language** — Use clear, concise sentences. Avoid complex syntax and unnecessary jargon. Maintain logical, predictable phrasing.

**Alt text** — Translate accurately. Keep it descriptive and concise. Focus on the essential meaning of the image — no unnecessary detail.

**Link text and CTAs** — Ensure all link text is descriptive, not vague. Avoid "Clique aqui" — describe the action:

> ✅ "Saiba mais sobre reembolsos"
> ❌ "Clique aqui"

**Dates, numbers, and currencies** — Apply pt-BR regional formats (see Section 8).

**Idioms and ambiguous language** — Avoid idioms, metaphors, or culturally specific references that may not translate or could confuse screen reader users.

### 10.3 Brand promise

The tagline **"Fly smart, enjoy more"** is a protected brand asset. Do not translate it. When it appears in copy, keep it in English.

## 11. Do's and don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Use informal "você" throughout B2C copy | Use "tu" or overly formal constructions |
| Use active/imperative voice in UI and instructional copy | Use passive constructions where active is available |
| Use contractions in informal and semi-formal contexts | Avoid contractions in legal or formal contexts |
| Keep sentences short and clear — one idea per sentence | Write long, complex sentences |
| Use facts and figures to support claims | Use vague superlatives without evidence |
| Apply transcreation for promotional/creative copy | Translate promotional copy word for word |
| Stay close to source for transactional/instructional copy | Transcreate UI strings or legal notices |
| Keep all brand names in English | Translate GoToGate, Mytrip, or Flight Network |
| Use the % symbol | Write "por cento" |
| Write all numbers as digits | Spell out numbers |
| Use the 24-hour clock with colon: 14:00 | Use 12-hour AM/PM format |
| Place currency code before amount: R$ 50 | Place currency code after amount |
| Use gender-neutral plural constructions | Use "ele/ela" where avoidable |
| Use descriptive link text and CTA copy | Use "Clique aqui" or vague link text |
| Note transcreation decisions in Smartling comments | Apply creative deviations without explanation |
