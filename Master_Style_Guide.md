**MASTER STYLE GUIDE**

*Consolidated Guidance Across All Accounts*

2024 Edition

How to use this guide

Section 1 — Common Standards consolidates guidance that appears consistently across the majority of style guides reviewed. Apply these defaults whenever an account-specific rule does not override them.

Section 2 — Account-Specific Instructions contains rules unique to, or significantly different from, each individual account. Always consult the relevant account section before delivering work.

**SECTION 1**

## Common Standards

The following guidelines reflect shared expectations across the majority of accounts in this portfolio. Unless an account-specific section states otherwise, these rules apply.

### 1.1  Voice &amp; Tone

Most accounts call for a professional, approachable tone. The most common baseline is:

- Clear and confident — avoid hedging, filler phrases, and jargon unless the audience is clearly technical.
- Helpful without being condescending — assume the reader is intelligent; introduce terms as a reminder, not a first lesson.
- Human and accessible — write like a knowledgeable colleague, not a legal document.

Tone varies by context (marketing vs. UI copy vs. legal text) but the underlying voice remains consistent within each account.

### 1.2  Second-Person Pronoun ("You")

The majority of accounts specify formal use of "you." A smaller group of accounts — typically consumer-facing apps and lifestyle brands — use the informal register. See the table below, and account-specific sections for exact instructions.

| **Category**              | **Formal "You" (majority)**                                     | **Informal "You" (minority)**                        |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| B2B / Enterprise Software | Bazaarvoice, Equinix, Fidelity, Fountain, Keeper, KeepIt, Vanta | —                                                    |
| HR / Consulting           | —                                                               | Eskalera, Globoforce                                 |
| Consumer / Lifestyle      | —                                                               | Fox Racing, Swarm, Mailchimp, Schwab, Lucid Software |
| Travel                    | —                                                               | Eurail (informal; legal pages formal)                |

### 1.3  Active Voice

Active voice is strongly preferred across all accounts. A small number of accounts explicitly call for passive voice in certain contexts:

- Equinix — passive voice preferred throughout.
- Keeper Security — passive voice preferred.
- Schwab — passive voice encouraged.

For all other accounts, write in the active voice. Passive may be used sparingly to soften negative messaging.

### 1.4  Colloquial Expressions

The clear majority of accounts mark colloquial expressions as inappropriate. Exceptions are limited to consumer-facing and lifestyle accounts:

- Appropriate: Fox Racing, Swarm, Lucid Software, Eskalera (approachable but professional).
- Inappropriate: All other accounts in this portfolio.

When in doubt, lean formal. Avoid slang, idioms, and pop-culture references unless the account explicitly permits them.

### 1.5  Abbreviations

Practice varies by account. The common rule across all accounts is: spell out an abbreviation or acronym on first use, followed by the short form in parentheses. After that, the short form alone may be used.

- Not acceptable (avoid abbreviations): Bazaarvoice, Equinix, Eskalera, Globoforce, KeepIt, Mailchimp, Vanta.
- Acceptable: Fidelity, Fountain, Fox Racing, Keeper, Lucid Software, Schwab, Sovos, Swarm, Ironclad.
- Abbreviations must end in a period where they are accepted: Keeper, Schwab, KeepIt, Mailchimp.
- Do not use periods after units of measure (g, km, m): Fountain, Fox Racing, Keeper, Lucid Software.

### 1.6  Do Not Translate

The following element types appear on every account's protected list. These must never be translated or localized:

- Branded names and product names
- Website names
- Usernames
- Email addresses
- URLs

Additional protected categories appear in some accounts — see Section 2 for account-specific additions (e.g., numbers, currencies, dates, units of measure).

### 1.7  Bullet Points

Consistent bullet-point formatting applies across the large majority of accounts:

- Begin each bullet point with a capital letter.
- If a bullet point is a complete sentence, end it with a period.
- If a bullet point is a fragment, no closing punctuation is needed (unless otherwise specified).

Accounts that omit a rule for incomplete bullets follow the same convention by default.

### 1.8  Headings &amp; Capitalization

Sentence case is the most common standard for headings (capitalize only the first word and proper nouns). Title case applies only where explicitly required by the account. Key rules:

- Do not add a period at the end of headings.
- Do not capitalize words mid-sentence unless they are proper nouns or branded terms.
- Branded Ironclad terms are capitalized when referring to a specific feature; lowercase when used generically.
- KeepIt: never use title case in headings or UI elements.

### 1.9  Numbers

Most accounts follow AP Style conventions unless noted in the account section:

- Spell out numbers one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above.
- Use numerals before units of measure (e.g., 5 km).
- Use commas as thousand separators and periods as decimal separators (e.g., 1,000.50) — unless the account targets a non-English locale.

### 1.10  Inclusive &amp; Accessible Language

Multiple accounts explicitly address inclusivity. Apply these standards broadly:

- Avoid slang, idioms, and discriminatory language.
- Use "they/them" as a singular pronoun when gender is unknown or irrelevant.
- Do not rely on sensory descriptors (shape, color, position) as the only navigational cue.
- Aim for a reading level of grade 8 where a broad audience is addressed (Eskalera, Schwab, Eurail).

### 2.4  Eurail / Interrail (Brazilian Portuguese)

| **Industry**        | Rail Travel / E-Commerce                                             |
|---------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Source Language** | English                                                              |
| **Target Language** | Brazilian Portuguese (PT-BR)                                         |
| **Audience**        | Non-European travelers unfamiliar with train travel; broad age range |
| **Reading Level**   | Grade 8                                                              |

#### Voice &amp; Register

- Informal, friendly, and light — "happy holiday" atmosphere for marketing content.
- Legal and official pages (Terms, Privacy Policy, Booking Terms, Disclaimer, etc.) must use formal style with no contractions.
- Use "você / vocês" to address the audience.
- Inspirational content (itineraries, top-10 pages) may be creative; informational content (train/product info) should stay close to source.

#### Product Name Rules

- Always capitalize product names; never translate product names (e.g., Eurail Italy Pass, Global Pass, One Country Pass).
- "Pass" (capital P) when referring to a specific Eurail or Interrail pass = do not translate.
- "pass" (lowercase p) = translate as "passe" (PT-BR).
- Pass types (Child Pass, Youth Pass, Adult Pass, Continuous Pass) can be translated; on first mention include English in brackets.
- Never translate "pass" as "ticket" — they are different products.
- Reservation fee (railway carrier charge) and Booking fee (Eurail website charge) must be translated distinctly.
- Never use the word "coverage" translated as "seguro" in Pass Loss &amp; Theft contexts. Never use acronym "LTC".
- Rail Planner: capitalize; do not translate; "App" may be translated.

#### Formatting (PT-BR)

- Capitalization: Sentence case only; no unnecessary capitalization; headings: first word only. Months and days in lowercase. No uppercase after colon unless proper noun.
- Numbers: Comma as decimal separator, period as thousands separator (e.g., 1.000,00). Ordinals in superscript (1ª, 2º).
- Dates: Day month year format (20 de junho de 2012). Abbreviated: 20/06/2012.
- Currency: Symbol before number, separated by non-breaking space.
- URLs: No accent marks, uppercase letters, or special characters; use hyphens.
- Place names: Follow with local name in brackets — "Basílica de São Pedro (Basilica Papale di San Pietro)".
- Buttons/CTAs: Use imperative verb form; infinitive if space-constrained.
- Acronyms: Full term + acronym in parentheses on first mention per page.

### 2.9  Ironclad

| **Industry**   | Legal Tech / Contract Lifecycle Management                                                           |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Audience**   | Legal ops professionals, attorneys, and business users — experts and non-experts                     |
| **Voice/Tone** | Smart but not stuffy; helpful but not condescending; friendly but not goofy; confident but not cocky |

#### UX Writing Principles

- Focus on "you" not "we" — center on what users can do, not what features exist.
- Write for non-technical audiences; use descriptive headlines and subheadings.
- Be direct, clear, and specific. Short words and sentences. Use contractions naturally.
- Assume their intelligence — introduce terms as a reminder, not a first lesson.

#### Voice (1st vs. 3rd Person)

- Instructive text / system messages: third person — "There are 3 unread messages in your inbox."
- Actionable elements / buttons: first person — "View my inbox."

#### Specific Rules

- Active voice throughout.
- Sentence case for headings, buttons, menu items, navigation.
- Section/table headings (eyebrows): ALL CAPS.
- Do not capitalize after a slash unless a proper noun: "Email/phone number."
- Ironclad-branded feature names (Editor, Workflow Designer, Repository, Smart Import) are always capitalized. Do not capitalize generic legal terms.
- "Redline(s)" is one word always.
- "e-signature" and "e-sign" use a hyphen; keep lowercase unless starting a sentence.
- Avoid permissive language: "Send emails directly to this workflow" not "You can send emails..."
- Use "please" only when the user is asked to do something inconvenient due to Ironclad's error.
- Don't describe UI elements — describe actions and goals instead.
- Use inclusive language; avoid "blacklist/whitelist" — prefer "blocklist/grantlist".
- Microcopy: avoid articles where possible ("Start workflow" not "Start a workflow").
- Numbers: numerals in most cases; spell out when mixed with other spelled-out numbers.
- Money: dollar sign + numeral; spell out million/billion/trillion.
- Dates: spell out day and month (Thursday, July 26).
- Time: numerals with AM/PM; space between (7 AM, 7:30 PM); default time zone is PT.
- Oxford comma required in lists.
- Telephone: dashes without spaces (555-867-5309).
- Reference the AP Style Guide for anything not covered above.

#### URL Localization Rules

The pt-BR language code is **`pt`**. Do not include special characters, accents, or umlauts in translated URL slugs. When a URL appears as a slug only (e.g., `/use-cases/project-planning`), apply the same rules as for the full URL of that type. Translate all instances of the same URL consistently within a job.

| Type | URL type | Translate slug? | Add `pt`? | Position of `pt` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Images | No | No | — (leave exactly as in source) |
| 2 | In-product templates | No | Yes | After `lucid.app/`, before product name |
| 3 | Pricing pages | No | Yes | After `lucid.app/`, before `/pricing/` |
| 4 | Login pages | No | Yes | After `lucid.app/`, before `/users/` |
| 5 | Training Labs | No | No | — (leave exactly as in source) |
| 6 | Use-case pages | Yes | Yes | After `.../pages/` (Lucidchart) · after domain (Lucidspark) |
| 7 | Blog posts | Yes | Yes | After domain before `/blog/` (Lucidspark · Lucid.co) · after `/blog/` (Lucidchart) |
| 8 | Marketing templates | Yes | No | — (translate slug string only) |
| 9 | PPC landing pages | Yes | Yes | After domain, before `/landing/` |
| 10 | Help Center articles | No (pre-translated) | Yes (find existing) | After `/hc/`, replacing `en-us` with `pt` |
| 11 | Other marketing pages | Yes | Yes | After `.../pages/` (Lucidchart) · after domain (others) |

For formulas and worked examples for each URL type, see the standalone `Lucid_URL_Localization_Rules_ptBR.md`.
